true

Abstract

Multiplex polymerase chain reaction (multiplex PCR) is a foundational enrichment technology for targeted next-generation sequencing (NGS), clinical pathogen panels, and forensic identification. However, high-plex reactions suffer from non-specific primer-dimer formation, which can monopolize sequencing throughput and deplete on-target amplification. In 2022, Xie et al. introduced SADDLE (Simulated Annealing Design using Dimer Likelihood Estimation, Nature Communications, 13, 1881; PMID: 35410464), describing an empirical heuristic scoring framework and simulated annealing algorithm to design multiplex primer pools up to 384-plex. While the experimental utility of SADDLE was demonstrated in the original paper, the validation sequencing analysis relied on proprietary MATLAB scripts coupled to external command-line aligners, while the simulated annealing design code remained proprietary under patent restrictions. In this work, following the ReScience C guidelines for computational replication, we conduct an independent replication of the central empirical NGS validation finding of Xie et al.: the read classification breakdown and primer-interaction distribution of the SADDLE-designed 384-plex validation panel across 3,942,385 raw paired-end sequencing reads (Sample E19; Figure 3f). Using an independent, open-source Python reimplementation, our pipeline classifies 2,860,658 reads into 43.86% on-target amplicons, 55.27% nonspecific products, and 0.87% primer-dimers, reproducing the published proportions (~43%, ~56%, ~1%) within a 1.0% absolute tolerance. We further corroborate the severe heavy-tailed concentration of primer-dimer formation across the 295,296 candidate primer pairings, where only 1,172 pairs (0.397%) exhibit non-zero dimerization and a single interaction (rp213 × rp371) accounts for 67.57% of all dimer reads (16,805 reads). In a supplementary exploratory benchmark, we evaluate the predictive validity of the SADDLE Badness heuristic against nearest-neighbor thermodynamics and supervised machine learning models, documenting an Average Precision of 0.0597 (a 4.8-fold improvement over nearest-neighbor free energy alone) while analyzing the severe probability miscalibration of supervised baselines (Brier scores ~0.20 vs ~0.0040 baseline). All replication code, derived datasets, and an automated verification script that executes in under two seconds are openly accessible under permissive licenses.

Introduction

Multiplex PCR simultaneously amplifies dozens to hundreds of genomic loci within a single reaction volume, serving as an essential preparation step for targeted clinical diagnostics, comprehensive cancer hotspot panels, and metagenomic surveillance (Untergasser et al. 2012; Chen et al. 2026). However, scaling multiplex PCR beyond small panels is fundamentally constrained by pairwise primer-primer cross-hybridization. For a panel targeting N amplicons, 2N distinct primer oligonucleotides are pooled, yielding:

$$\binom{2N+1}{2} = \frac{(2N+1)(2N)}{2} \approx 2N^2$$

possible pairwise interaction combinations (including homodimer and self-interactions). Minor sequence complementarity—specifically when aligned at the extensible 3’ terminus—enables DNA polymerase extension, producing short artifactual amplicons termed primer-dimers (Xie et al. 2022). Due to their substantially smaller size relative to target genomic amplicons, primer-dimers amplify with high kinetic efficiency, rapidly exhausting enzymatic reagents and saturating sequencing libraries.

To mitigate this exponential risk, Xie et al. (2022) introduced SADDLE (Simulated Annealing Design using Dimer Likelihood Estimation), proposing an empirical biophysical scoring heuristic called Badness to quantify 3’-directed extensible hybridization and using simulated annealing optimization to partition multiplex primer pools into compatible sub-pools. In their 96-plex experimental optimization (Figures 3a–3c), the authors demonstrated that primer-dimer reads dropped from 90.7% in a naively designed pool (PS1) to 4.9% in an optimized pool (PS3). Scaling the technology to higher multiplexing, the authors designed and experimentally tested a 384-plex primer set designed by SADDLE (768 primers; Sample E19). In Figure 3f and the accompanying text, the authors reported that the sequencing library of this 384-plex panel achieved high on-target specificity (~43%) and low primer-dimer formation (~1%), with the remaining reads (~56%) representing nonspecific background products aligning across the human genome via non-cognate primer pairs.

Despite the impact of SADDLE, two key reproducibility hurdles exist: 1. Proprietary Software Barriers: The simulated annealing software used to design primer sets was withheld under intellectual property and patent restrictions (Xie et al. 2022). Furthermore, the empirical NGS validation scripts deposited on Figshare were implemented in proprietary MATLAB and required external aligners (Langmead and Salzberg 2012), preventing turnkey, open-source execution. 2. Independent Verification of Empirical Partitions: While the authors published rounded percentage summaries in Figure 3f of their manuscript, an independent, open-source replication of the read-accounting pipeline operating directly on the raw FASTQ data has not been formally documented.

In this ReScience C article (Rougier et al. 2017), we report an independent computational replication focused strictly on the empirical validation analysis of Xie et al. (2022): - Primary Replication Objective: Does an independent, open-source Python implementation replicate the published read classification proportions (~43% on-target, ~56% nonspecific, ~1% primer-dimer) across all 3,942,385 raw paired-end reads of the SADDLE 384-plex validation panel (Sample E19, Figure 3f), and does it reproduce the heavy-tailed concentration of primer-dimer reads? - Implementation Mapping: Can the read-classification pipeline be reconstructed without proprietary MATLAB dependencies or external binary alignment tools while maintaining operational fidelity? - Supplementary Exploratory Analysis: In a secondary analysis clearly distinguished from the primary replication claim, how well does the SADDLE Badness heuristic rank observed dimer events compared to standard nearest-neighbor thermodynamics ( − ΔG) and supervised machine learning models?

Original Work and Artifact Audit

The original study by Xie et al. (2022) was published in Nature Communications (Volume 13, Article 1881; PMID: 35410464). Primer sequences, genomic coordinates, and assay details were provided in Supplementary Data (MOESM5). The raw paired-end sequencing reads for the 384-plex validation panel (Sample E19, Illumina MiniSeq 2 × 150 bp) and post-hoc analysis scripts (Data_process.m, Dimer_Identify.m, analysis_code.ipynb) were deposited on Figshare (DOI: 10.6084/m9.figshare.16944154.v3) under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International license (CC BY 4.0).

Our audit of the deposited artifacts identified: - Design Code: The simulated annealing optimization algorithm was not included in the public deposit, with the manuscript stating: “The code for SADDLE primer design is available upon request for non-commercial academic use” subject to patent protection. Consequently, the design algorithm itself is out of scope for an open, autonomous replication. - Validation Pipeline: The public analysis workflow comprises MATLAB scripts that trim Illumina adapters, filter reads by Phred quality and length, align reads to reference amplicons using Bowtie2 (Langmead and Salzberg 2012), match 5’ primer sequences via an 8-base hash lookup, and classify reads with insert lengths strictly shorter than L1 + L2 − 2 bp as primer-dimers. - Raw Data Completeness: The Figshare repository contains the complete 3,942,385 raw paired-end reads for Sample E19 across two gzipped FASTQ files (E19_384plex_NGS_R1_fastq.gz and E19_384plex_NGS_R2_fastq.gz, totaling 914 MB), providing uncompromised primary empirical inputs.

Replication Methodology and Implementation

We implemented a clean-room, open-source Python 3.10+ pipeline designed to operate without MATLAB or platform-dependent aligners.

Read Accounting and Partition Logic

Each paired-end sequencing read (R1,R2) in Sample E19 is processed through mutually exclusive and exhaustive accounting bins: 1. Adapter Missing: Read pairs lacking adapter signatures on both reads, R1 only, or R2 only. 2. Quality Excluded: Read pairs with trimmed inserts  ≤ 10 bp, containing ambiguous 'N' bases, or failing the 70% Q30 threshold. 3. Primer Unmatched: Read pairs where either or both 5’ ends cannot be matched to the 768 pool primers with  > 60% identity. 4. Classified Amplicons: Reads with both primers successfully resolved. These are partitioned into Primer-Dimers (insert  < L1 + L2 − 2 bp), and non-dimer products. To evaluate on-target specificity rigorously, non-dimer products are assessed under two distinct methodological criteria: - Method 1 (Primer Pairing Heuristic): A read is classified as On-Target if both primers form a cognate target pair (P2 = P1 + 384) and insert length  ≥ L1 + L2 − 2 bp; all non-cognate pairings are classified as Nonspecific. This turnkey method avoids external aligner dependencies. - Method 2 (Genuine Gapped Sequence Alignment via edlib): In addition to cognate primer matching, the insert sequence is aligned directly against the intended full-length reference amplicon sequence extracted from Supplementary Data MOESM5 (Xie et al. 2022) using the open-source edlib C/Python library (Myers’ exact bit-vector semi-global alignment with indels and substitutions, mode='SHW'). Reads achieving  ≥ 75% or  ≥ 80% sequence identity against the intended amplicon are classified as On-Target; reads failing this identity threshold are classified as Nonspecific.

Primary Replication Results

Replicated NGS Read Accounting Breakdown

Executing the read-accounting pipeline across all 3,942,385 raw paired-end reads of Sample E19 classifies 2,860,658 to 2,860,664 reads (72.56% of total raw sequencing depth), with 1,081,721 to 1,081,727 reads (27.44%) excluded. Exclusions partition into: - Missing adapter sequence: 751,644 reads (19.07% of raw depth); - Quality and length filters: 137,086 reads (3.48% of raw depth); - Primer prefix unmatched: 192,991 to 192,997 reads (4.90% of raw depth).

(Note on the 6-Read Lineage Discrepancy: The minor difference of exactly 6 reads between accounting partitions arises from the denominator used during primer prefix matching. In extreme-short primer dimers with inserts of 12–14 bp, dividing matches by full primer length ( ≈ 22 bp) yields identity  ≤ 0.60 and excludes the reads as primer-unmatched [192,997 excluded, 24,872 dimers], whereas dividing by the trimmed insert length evaluates identity as 1.0 and retains them as dimers [192,991 excluded, 24,878 dimers]. This single-read lineage difference does not affect any published percentage).

Biological and Methodological Insights

  1. Invariance of Primer-Dimer Suppression: Under all classification definitions, primer-dimer formation accounts for exactly 0.87% of classified reads, replicating the published finding of ~1% in Figure 3f within 0.13% absolute difference. Because primer-dimers are defined strictly by insert length truncation ( < L1 + L2 − 2 bp), dimer quantification is completely robust to down-stream sequence alignment criteria.
  2. Impact of Genuine Gapped Alignment vs. Positional String Matching: In earlier exploratory audits, evaluating reference sequence matching via ungapped character-by-character string comparison artificially depressed on-target classification to 39.49% (1,129,696 reads). Diagnostic inspection revealed that 1–2 bp sequencing indels or homopolymer stutter in MiniSeq reads caused downstream sequence frames to shift, artificially failing rigid unaligned positional checks. When genuine gapped semi-global alignment is applied using edlib, these indel-containing reads align with high fidelity, restoring on-target classification to 43.61% ( ≥ 75% identity, Δ =  + 0.61%) and 42.76% ( ≥ 80% identity, Δ =  − 0.24%). Both aligners quantitatively replicate the published ~43% on-target and ~56% nonspecific fractions within 0.6% absolute tolerance.
  3. Turnkey Replication Concordance: Both Method 1 and Method 2 confirm the central empirical claim of SADDLE: multiplex PCR libraries designed under SADDLE’s simulated annealing Badness minimization achieve  ≈ 43% on-target specificity while rigorously suppressing primer-dimer amplification to under 1%.

Extreme Heavy-Tailed Interaction Concentration

We next analyzed the distribution of the observed primer-dimer reads across all 295,296 possible candidate primer pairs: - Zero-Dimer Pairs: 294,124 candidate pairs (99.6031%) produced exactly zero observed dimer reads. - Active Dimer Pairs ( ≥ 1 read): Only 1,172 pairs (0.3969% empirical prevalence) generated one or more dimer reads. - Severe Dimer Pairs ( ≥ 10 reads): Only 98 pairs (0.0332% prevalence) produced 10 or more dimer reads. - Dominant Interaction: A single primer pair—reverse primer 213 (rp213) × reverse primer 371 (rp371)—generated 16,805 dimer reads, accounting for 67.57% of all primer-dimer reads in the entire library.

This reproduces the qualitative finding highlighted by Xie et al.: primer-dimer formation is not uniformly distributed across sequence space, but is characterized by an extreme, heavy-tailed concentration where a tiny minority of catastrophic pairs account for the vast majority of artifactual reads.

Supplementary Exploratory Analysis: Heuristic and Baseline Benchmarking

Note: The following benchmark was not published by Xie et al. (2022). It is conducted here as a secondary exploration to evaluate how the biophysical SADDLE Badness heuristic and machine learning models correlate with empirical dimer labels derived from Sample E19 across all 295,296 candidate primer pairs.

Formulation of SADDLE Badness and Baselines

In Xie et al. (2022), Badness is calculated over maximal continuous complementary matches of length  ≥ 4 bp between primers P1 and P2:

$$\text{Badness}(P_1, P_2) = \sum_{k \in \mathcal{M}} 2^{\text{len}_k} \cdot 2^{\text{numGC}_k} \cdot \frac{1}{(d_1^{(k)} + 1)(d_2^{(k)} + 1)}$$

where lenk is match length, numGCk is GC count within the match, and d1(k), d2(k) are nucleotide distances from the 3’ termini. In our benchmark, we evaluate an approximate 4–7 bp heuristic variant.

We compare Badness against: 1. SantaLucia Thermodynamics ( − ΔG): Hybridization free energy calculated at 60 °C using unified nearest-neighbor parameters (SantaLucia 1998). 2. Logistic Regression (Physics Features): L2-regularized linear model trained on thermodynamic and alignment features. 3. XGBoost (Physics Features): Gradient boosted decision tree ensemble trained on the same feature set.

To ensure deterministic evaluation without tie-breaking instability, candidates are ranked by primary score descending, with ties broken by ascending pair index: rank_key(i) = (−scorei,pair_indexi).

Findings from Supplementary Benchmarking

  1. Heuristic vs. Thermodynamics: SADDLE Badness achieves an Average Precision of 0.0597, representing a 4.8-fold improvement over nearest-neighbor free energy alone ( − ΔG, AP = 0.0125) and a 4.0-fold improvement in Precision@100 (24.0% vs. 6.0%). This supports the hypothesis of Xie et al. that terminal 3’ alignment and heavy GC penalties capture enzymatic extension risk more effectively than global hybridization stability.
  2. Top-Rank Discrimination vs. Supervised Models: SADDLE Badness outperforms both Logistic Regression (AP = 0.0205, P@100 = 8.0%) and XGBoost (AP = 0.0371, P@100 = 16.0%) in top-rank precision, demonstrating that simple biophysical heuristics remain competitive at the extreme head of the distribution.
  3. Severe Probability Miscalibration of Supervised Models: Independent recalculation of Brier scores reveals that both Logistic Regression (0.1986) and XGBoost (0.2143) are severely miscalibrated relative to the uninformative baseline (p(1−p) = 0.003953 ≈ 0.0040). Because the supervised models were trained to maximize ranking separation under class imbalance, their mean predicted probabilities (~0.38) greatly exceed the true empirical prevalence (0.00397), yielding large quadratic calibration penalties.

Methodological Disclosures

  1. Dual Evaluation: Primer Pairing vs. Genuine Gapped Sequence Alignment: Rather than relying solely on primer-pairing heuristics or unaligned string comparisons, our replication evaluated both classification paradigms across all 3,942,385 reads:
    • Method 1 (Primer Pairing): Assigns on-target status based on cognate primer IDs (P2 = P1 + 384) and insert length threshold  ≥ L1 + L2 − 2 bp, yielding 43.86% on-target, 55.27% nonspecific, and 0.87% primer-dimers. This reproduces published Figure 3f (~43% / ~56% / ~1%) within 1.0% absolute tolerance.
    • Method 2 (Genuine Gapped Sequence Alignment via edlib): Performs Myers’ exact bit-vector semi-global sequence alignment against all 384 intended reference amplicons from Supplementary Data MOESM5 (Xie et al. 2022). At  ≥ 75% identity, reads partition into 43.61% on-target, 55.52% nonspecific, and 0.87% primer-dimers (Δ =  + 0.61%). At  ≥ 80% identity, reads partition into 42.76% on-target, 56.37% nonspecific, and 0.87% primer-dimers (Δ =  − 0.24%). Both aligner cutoffs match published Figure 3f within scientific tolerance.
    • Positional vs Gapped Alignment Insight: Naive ungapped string matching artificially depresses on-target reads to 39.49% due to 1–2 bp sequencing indels, whereas genuine gapped alignment robustly tolerates sequencing variations. Crucially, primer-dimer quantification (0.87% vs ~1% published) is completely invariant across all methods.
    • Alignment Scope Distinction: In the original SADDLE publication (Xie et al. 2022), reads were aligned genome-wide using Bowtie2. In our lightweight, turnkey Python implementation, Method 2 executes cognate-anchored semi-global gapped alignment (edlib, Myers’ bit-vector algorithm) against the 384 intended target amplicons from MOESM5. Both approaches yield concordant results (~43% on-target, ~56% nonspecific, ~1% primer-dimers).
  2. Scope Limitation: This replication focuses strictly on the empirical validation read partitions and heuristic scoring. The proprietary simulated annealing optimization algorithm remains withheld under patent restrictions and is not evaluated.
  3. Distributional Terminology: We characterize the distribution of primer-dimer read counts as heavy-tailed and highly concentrated; no formal power-law distribution fit (e.g., Clauset-Shalizi-Newman methodology) was conducted.

Conclusion

We have performed an independent computational replication of the empirical NGS validation results from SADDLE (Xie et al. 2022). Using an open-source Python workflow, we confirmed that classified reads from the SADDLE-designed 384-plex validation panel (Figure 3f) replicate published values (~43% on-target, ~56% nonspecific, ~1% primer-dimers). Under primer-pairing classification (Method 1), reads partition into 43.86% on-target, 55.27% nonspecific, and 0.87% primer-dimers. Under genuine gapped sequence alignment against the 384 intended target amplicons (Method 2 via edlib), reads partition into 43.61% on-target ( ≥ 75% identity) or 42.76% on-target ( ≥ 80% identity), 55.52% to 56.37% nonspecific, and 0.87% primer-dimers, reproducing the published ~43% / ~56% / ~1% figures within 0.6% absolute tolerance. Under all criteria, primer-dimer formation is strictly suppressed at 0.87%, confirming the core experimental claim of SADDLE. We also corroborated the severe heavy-tailed concentration of dimer reads, with a single primer pair generating 67.57% of all dimer reads. Finally, our supplementary exploratory benchmark illustrated the predictive utility of the SADDLE Badness heuristic over standard thermodynamics while documenting the calibration characteristics of supervised models.

All data, scripts, and a standalone verification suite are available in the public replication repository.
